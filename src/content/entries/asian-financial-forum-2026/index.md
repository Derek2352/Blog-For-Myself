## What it was

The **Asian Financial Forum** is Hong Kong's opening financial event of the year, co-organised by
the HKSAR Government and the Hong Kong Trade Development Council. The 19th edition ran on 26 and 27
January 2026 at the Convention and Exhibition Centre in Wan Chai, under the theme *Co-creating New
Horizons Amid an Evolving Landscape* (<span lang="zh-Hant">變局中協力　新局中多贏</span>). Over two
days it drew more than 4,000 government and business leaders from over 60 countries and regions,
with more than 150 speakers.

This year added something new on the second day: the **Global Business Summit**
(<span lang="zh-Hant">全球產業峰會</span>), co-organised by the Financial Services and the Treasury
Bureau, HKTDC and the Office for Attracting Strategic Enterprises. Its brief was to take the forum
past finance itself and into the industries finance serves: mainland enterprises going global,
foreign firms entering the mainland, and panels on biomedicine, green energy, consumer markets, and
AI and robotics.

## What I did

I went on the second day, 27 January, on a Global Business Summit networking pass issued through
HSUHK, and stayed for almost the whole day. Three stages ran at once, so I moved between halls in
the afternoon. The four sessions in the gallery are the ones I photographed: two Global Business Summit panels on The Stage in Hall 5B, then two thematic
workshops on The Pulse in Hall 5E, the last of which finished at six.

### The room's priorities, in one poll

The forum polled the audience live. More than 70% were neutral to optimistic about the global
economy in 2026. Asked what should come first in the current international landscape, 51.2% chose
AI and AI-driven applications, and 20.3% chose the energy transition and sustainable development.

### Biomedicine 2026: trends, challenges and opportunities

*14:30–15:30, The Stage.* Speakers from Amgen and Merck, among them Dr Paul Burton, Amgen's Chief
Medical Officer, and Marc Horn, President of Merck China, put the industry's problem in financial
terms: research and development is expensive, and the
road from discovery to a product is long. Their ask of the finance sector was for more diversified
and more international channels of funding, so that capital can shorten the time it takes a
discovery to reach patients.

### AI infrastructure: powering the intelligent supply chain

*15:30–16:30, The Stage.* With Prof Yang Hongxia of PolyU's Department of Computing on the panel
alongside speakers from DexForce Technology, JD.com and Pictet Group, the session covered how AI
applications in the supply chain can be scaled, where the investment priorities lie, and how
companies grow by working with partners across an ecosystem.

### Building the financial stack for the AI economy

*16:15–16:45, The Pulse.* Cyberport ran this one, with Victor Yim, its Head of AI and Data Science,
in the chair. The question was how payment infrastructure can
work as a digital corridor between markets. Michele Fung of LianLian Global described its global
payout service, built to cut the cost and delay of traditional cross-border payments, and how
LianLian uses AI in its operations, intelligent payment routing and automated FX. She called payment
infrastructure the strategic foundation for connecting global markets. Jeffery Tong of Finloop and
Steve Shen of Waffo took up the same two tools, AI-driven FX automation and smart routing, and what
they do for the speed and reliability of a transaction.

### Finance and technology for the silver economy

*17:00–18:00, The Pulse.* The day's last session, co-organised with Yicai and chaired by its Chief
Correspondent in Hong Kong, Hang Yu. On the panel were Elaine Coughlan of Atlantic Bridge, Zhihai
Diao, Head of International Wealth Management at CICC, and Rongsa Zhong, Vice President and Head of
China at Principal Financial Group. The title set finance and technology up as the two engines of
the silver economy's development.
