## What it was

A career advisory conversation with **Mr. Vincent Tam**, and I came to it with a
question I thought was straightforward: the CFA is the obvious credential for where I'm
heading, so what else is worth the years?

He is a good person to ask. Twenty-eight years across corporate banking and then the
corporate side — corporate development, governance, internal audit, risk, and talent —
and a professional-qualification list long enough to be its own paragraph: Chartered
Banker in the UK, Certified Banker in Hong Kong, FCPA, FCCA, Chartered MCIPD, and,
most relevant to me, **FHKSI** — a Fellow of the Hong Kong Securities and Investment
Institute. He mentors across university programmes and judges case competitions, and
was named Best HR Leader of the Year at the Best HR Awards in both 2023 and 2024.

So: roughly twenty qualifications, and his advice was not to go and collect more.

## What I did

I asked, and then mostly listened, and what came back was less a list than an order of
operations.

**Experience before credentials.** The first thing he pointed me at wasn't an exam at
all — virtual work-experience programmes like **Forage**, and trading-floor simulations
like **AmplifyME**: the kind of thing you can work through before anyone has offered
you an internship. The reasoning is blunt and correct. A half-finished credential says
very little in a room; a piece of work you can talk through says a lot.

**Then the licence, which is not the same kind of object as the CFA.** The Hong Kong
Securities and Investment Institute runs the **Licensing Examination for Securities and
Futures Intermediaries**, and it answers a different question entirely. The CFA is a
statement about analytical depth, portable anywhere. The HKSI licensing exam is the
regulatory qualification for being permitted to do the job *here*. I had been treating
credentials as one ladder; they're at least two, and they don't substitute for each
other. He also pointed out the practical part: **HKSI student membership carries a 50%
discount on selected examinations**, which is worth having while I still qualify for it.

**Then sustainability, as a genuine second axis.** Three routes he considers
worthwhile: EFFAS's **Certified ESG Analyst**, the CFA Institute's **Certificate in ESG
Investing**, and GARP's **Sustainability and Climate Risk** certificate.

**And then the part I hadn't known about at all.** All three are eligible under Hong
Kong's **Pilot Green and Sustainable Finance Capacity Building Support Scheme** — which
reimburses Hong Kong residents **up to HKD 10,000** on passing one of them. An ESG
qualification isn't a cost I'd been budgeting for; done in the right order, it's close
to free.

## What I learned

The question was wrong, and finding that out was the useful part. "What should I do
besides the CFA" assumes the credentials compete for the same slot. They don't. The
CFA answers *can this person analyse*. The HKSI licence answers *is this person
permitted to practise in this market*. An ESG certificate answers *can this person work
where the mandates are actually moving*. Three different questions from three different
audiences — so the real problem isn't choosing between them, it's sequencing them, and
sequencing has a right answer where choosing doesn't.

The second thing landed harder. **Being a student is a financial asset with an expiry
date.** A 50% examination discount and a HKD 10,000 government reimbursement are
available to me now, in a way they will not be in three years. I had been thinking of
this period as the part before the career starts. It's more accurate to think of it as
a window that is currently open.

And the thing I keep returning to: the man with twenty qualifications did not tell me to
get more qualifications. He told me to get experience I could talk about, take the
cheap exams while they're cheap, and let someone else pay for the expensive one. That
is what expertise sounds like when it isn't selling you anything.

There's a line that runs back through the rest of this site. After the Three Cities
trip I wrote that I wasn't training for "finance" but for AI-literate finance — CFA
alongside Python and R rather than instead of them. This conversation added the two
layers that framing was missing: the local licence that makes the work legal, and the
sustainability credential that decides which desks will want it.

## How it felt

Slightly humbling, in a way I didn't mind. I had come in wanting a shopping list and
left with a sequencing problem, which is a better problem and also a harder one —
lists you can tick, order you have to think about.

The part that stayed with me afterwards was the arithmetic. I went and worked out what
the discount and the reimbursement are actually worth, and the answer is: more than any
part-time job I could take in the same period. It's an odd feeling to realise the most
financially significant thing you can do this year is sit an exam at the right moment,
while you still count as a student.
