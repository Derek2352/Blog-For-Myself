## What it was

The **Directors' Symposium** is The Hong Kong Institute of Directors' yearly afternoon for its
members, billed as a forum designed by directors for directors. The 2026 edition ran on
**10 September** at the Ocean Park Marriott, from registration at 1:15 to closing remarks at five,
under the theme *Navigating Through Disruptive Forces in Challenging Times*, and counted for three
hours of CPD.

Six sessions filled the afternoon, opened and closed by HKIoD's Chairman, Richard Tsang: a keynote
from the Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau, a fireside chat on modern board leadership, a
panel on balancing AI innovation with governance, a talk on the economic outlook, and a closing panel
on directorship and what HKIoD called the longevity blueprint.

## What I did

I joined HKIoD as a member in 2026, and the symposium was my first event as one: I went on the
complimentary ticket the Institute gives new members. Most of the room were sitting directors; I was
there to listen.

### An overview of the economic outlook

**Samuel Tse**, Senior Economist and Market Strategist at DBS Bank (Hong Kong) and head of its
Hong Kong fixed income research, with HKIoD Deputy Chairman **William Lo** as host. He built the talk
around five themes:

- **Rates.** Geopolitical tension, and the pressure it puts on oil and inflation, is a reason to
  expect US interest rates to stay higher for longer.
- **The debasement trade.** America's growing debt and the cost of servicing it, and what that does
  to how investors price the dollar.
- **The renminbi.** Its use in international payments and the flow of foreign money into China's
  bonds have both grown since the US–Iran war began, and he credited the currency's relative
  strength to China's trade surplus, its energy resilience and that wider use.
- **China to 2040.** A long-range growth outlook built on the Solow growth model, weighing an ageing
  population and the property downturn against AI and robotics, with base, bull and bear cases. He
  described the economy as K-shaped: coastal hubs, leading tech firms and AI-led manufacturers
  pulling away from inland regions, smaller firms, young graduates and labour-intensive industry.
- **Hong Kong.** The case for the city as a wealth management centre and a hub for fixed income and
  currencies: offshore renminbi and Dim Sum bonds, regional headquarters, and family offices.

### Directorship and the longevity blueprint

The closing panel, hosted by HKIoD's Immediate Past Chairman, **Dr Christopher To**, with
**Richard Ho** of Texwinca Holdings, **Brian Leung** of Woo Kwan Lee & Lo and **Grégoire Michaud**,
founder of Bakehouse. The subject was how organisations last: staying alive over the long run through
purpose and digital transformation.

The room was polled live on it. Asked whether their organisation had prepared and put in place a
longevity blueprint, 21 people answered. The largest group, 29%, had not yet considered or discussed
one; 24% had one running only in certain departments or pilots; and 19% said theirs was fully in
operation across the organisation.
