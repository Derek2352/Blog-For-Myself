## What it was

A selected-delegate exchange into the heart of Central China's tech build-out, supported by
Lingnan University, Saint Francis University, HYAB and YDC. Where my other study tours
followed the money and the market, this one followed the **hardware** — the physical
infrastructure that AI and modern computing actually run on, in **Wuhan** and **Xiangyang**.

## What I did

The delegation went straight to the machines. At the **Wuhan AI Computing Center** and
**Supercomputing Center**, I saw the compute layer up close — the AI centre runs on Huawei's
domestic **Atlas 900** cluster (Ascend silicon, ~100 PFLOPS at launch), part of China's push
to build a home-grown AI stack under a very different supply chain from the one I take for
granted. At the **Wuhan National Laboratory for Optoelectronics** at
HUST — one of the country's first national laboratories, working on photonics, integrated
optics and next-generation memory — the exchange turned to the frontier of the physical
science beneath the silicon. The trip then widened into Central China's broader landscape
through Xiangyang's municipal science museums and the **Hubei Longzhong Laboratory**,
pairing frontier tech with the region's history and culture.

## What I learned

Standing in front of a supercomputing cluster reframes "the cloud" as what it really is:
buildings, power, cooling, and sovereign hardware choices. For someone coming from FinTech,
it was a useful correction — the AI I use as a service is somebody else's enormous physical
investment, and understanding that supply chain matters. Seeing optoelectronics research
next to applied AI infrastructure also joined two dots I usually hold apart: the long-horizon
science and the systems already in production.
