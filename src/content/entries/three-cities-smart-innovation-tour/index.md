## What it was

Eight days, three cities, under HSUHK's 「學貴有恒」 — *learning requires perseverance* — as a
selected participant. The three weren't picked for variety. They're three layers of the same
economy stacked on top of each other: **Shanghai** for corporate innovation and what a city
does with its old buildings, **Yiwu** for physical trade at a scale that has to be walked
through to be believed, and **Hangzhou** for the platforms and algorithms sitting above both.

## What I did

In Shanghai we sat with Deswell Group, a listed manufacturer, and with Youbao Info Tech and
Xijing Tech — the latter building B2B automation rather than anything consumer-facing. Then
Columbia Circle (上生·新所), which is a former industrial compound turned into a working
district rather than a museum of itself, and the Urban Planning Exhibition Hall, which is a
city explaining its own argument to itself.

Yiwu was the part that rearranged my thinking. The Cross-Border E-commerce Association, a
livestream commerce operation mid-broadcast, a logistics sorting centre running at a speed
that makes Hong Kong feel careful, and District 1 of the International Trade City — one
district, and you still don't finish it. Hangzhou closed the loop at Alibaba's headquarters
and its AI workshop.

Two frames came back with me. The first is **shelf commerce against content commerce**: Hong
Kong still largely sells from a static list you have to go and search, while Hangzhou and
Yiwu have moved to selling inside a scene you happen to be watching. The second is **人貨場 —
people, goods, scene** — where "goods" no longer means what a buyer's instinct says will sell.
Product selection runs off backend data, and the people doing it treat a hunch as a hypothesis
rather than a decision.

The part that belongs to my degree started with me not understanding something. A speaker in
Yiwu described SMEs struggling to get money back into the mainland, and my first reaction was
genuine confusion: there is a banking system, so why is this hard? Standing in front of people
who trade for a living, what I knew about FinTech felt 過於理論化 — too theoretical to touch
the problem.

So I asked. The chairman of the Cross-Border E-commerce Association walked through it with
worked examples, and the crux turned out to be foreign-exchange thresholds and the cost of
staying compliant — not banking capacity at all. Hong Kong's answer is unglamorous and
genuinely valuable: a compliant offshore RMB settlement route, legal infrastructure the
mainland market actually needs. That night I went back to the hotel and read up on cross-border
RMB capital pools and individual conversion quotas, because I wanted the answer to stay.

It also reshaped what I think I'm training for. Not "finance" but AI-literate finance — CFA
alongside Python and R rather than instead of them; supply-chain financing and settlement for
cross-border sellers; digital-transformation consulting for firms like Deswell, where the work
is connecting an ERP to a bank's API rather than anything with a token attached. The systems I
find myself wanting to build are the ones ordinary people end up relying on without ever
thinking about them.

Afterwards I turned the trip into a piece of work rather than a folder of photos: a
livestream-style video report for Careers Fair 2026, delivered to classmates as if reporting
back from the field — the three cities, the commerce shift, and where a Hong Kong finance
student actually fits.

## What I learned

The honest comparison runs both ways, and I'd rather write it that way than flatter either
side.

On the mainland, livestreaming, backend inventory and instant settlement are one motion rather
than three systems bolted together, and 產學結合 runs continuously from a vocational college to
the trade floor. Yiwu's cluster effect is a physical fact you can stand inside: put logistics,
suppliers and streamers in one district and the cost of a conversation collapses. Hong Kong
sells mostly from web and app listings, livestream selling is still early, and payment is split
across credit cards, FPS and Octopus — three rails where the mainland has one habit. We have
Cyberport and Science Park, but land prices keep a supply chain from ever sitting in one place,
so anything involving production goes across the border by default.

What Hong Kong holds is not small: rule of law, intellectual property protection, capital
mobility, an open internet, and a reputation that clears deals. That's the profile of a place
built for high-value, asset-light FinTech connecting overseas clients — which is a real role,
not a consolation. Neither list cancels the other, and the interesting work is in the seam.

Yiwu in particular reframed "digital economy" for me — behind every platform is still a
mountain of real goods and real traders, and the interesting FinTech questions live at that
seam between the physical and the digital.

## How it felt

The chairman put a question to us I haven't been able to put down:
「你是來學經驗還是來賺錢？這是兩碼事。」 *Are you here to learn, or here to earn? They're two
different things.* Starting pay in Yiwu is low, he said, but you learn how the entire supply
chain works — and at the start of a career you don't get to optimise for both.

That landed harder than I expected, because it named something I hadn't admitted. As a Hong
Kong business student it is easy to weigh a first job by its salary and its 光環, the halo of
the address. 蹲得低才跳得高 — crouch low to jump high. Watching people compete in that market
day after day, I also came away thinking my own adaptability and tolerance for pressure are
thinner than I'd assumed. Better to know that in year one than in year five.

The other thing I didn't expect to be writing down is the people. Eight days is long enough to
see who asks questions when the room has gone quiet, and the late-night sessions back at the
hotel — arguing about what we'd just seen — taught me as much as the visits did. 「學貴有恒」:
learning requires perseverance. I'm grateful to the leaders who ran it, and this reads to me
like a starting point rather than a trip I've finished.

Moving through three such different cities in quick succession — finance, trade, and tech —
left me with a bigger, more connected picture of the region I want to work in.
