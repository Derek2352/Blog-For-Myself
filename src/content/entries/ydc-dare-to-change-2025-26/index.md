## What it was

The **YDC Dare to Change Business Pitch Competition 2025–26**, where I competed as part of
**Team FORCES**. Our concept tackled a genuinely thorny question: the **technical
feasibility and ethical implementation of automated financial decision-making** — where
algorithms should and shouldn't be trusted to decide with people's money.

## What I did

I used LLMs and AI-driven analytics to **synthesise market trends and competitive
landscapes**, hunting for the specific gap a startup could credibly own — and used AI design
and productivity tools to iterate the pitch-deck visuals and market-entry strategy into a
high-impact presentation for a panel of experts. The through-line to my other work was the
*ethical* framing: not just *can* we automate a financial decision, but *should* we, and
under what safeguards.

## What I learned

Pitching sharpened a different muscle from building — the discipline of compressing a messy,
serious idea into something a panel grasps and believes in minutes. Sitting automation next
to ethics also taught me that in FinTech the responsible answer is often the more defensible
*business* answer too; trust is the product.
